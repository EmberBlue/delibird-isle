## Reading guide

Three load-bearing ideas, in order of weight:

1. **The ecology is legible — species are sentences.** Presence, absence,
   abundance, and condition of the right species *report* the health of a
   place. Wren's whole power is learning to read them. The survey is the
   gameplay; the species are the prose.
2. **Resilience is finite, and the lie is pretending it isn't.** Every
   ecosystem absorbs damage up to a threshold and then flips to a degraded
   state that **does not flip back** (§ The resilience crux). Dr. Heron's
   noble lie inflated a real-but-fragile recovery; Mereholt generalized that
   inflation into "ecosystems bounce back, so extraction is survivable" (§04,
   §05). The entire conflict is a fight over one scientific question:
   *how much can a system take before it doesn't come back?*
3. **Damage wears the mask of improvement** (§00, §02). Every disturbance in
   this game arrives dressed as a benefit — a culvert "upgrade," a "managed"
   preserve, "exploratory" drilling, "sustainable" harvest. The ecology is
   how the mask comes off: the signs in the species contradict the signs on
   the signposts.

## The ecological model (FIRMED)

The whole game runs on a small number of real, teachable ecological concepts.
Each one is chosen because it produces a **signal the player can observe** and
because it serves a narrative beat already locked elsewhere.

### Indicator species

Taxa whose presence, absence, or condition reports the state of an ecosystem.
The player learns to read four indicator guilds, each mapped to a real-world
bioindicator and to a recurring Pokémon role:

| Guild | Real-world role | Illustrative Pokémon | What it reports |
|-------|-----------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| **Amphibians** | water quality, connectivity (permeable skin = first to suffer) | Poliwag line, Wooper/Quagsire, Croagunk/Toxicroak | freshwater health; contamination; the *first* guild to vanish or deform |
| **Lichen / fungal** | air & moisture quality (lichens die in polluted air) | Foongus/Amoonguss, Morelull/Shiinotic, Breloom | air quality; old-growth moisture regime; forest integrity |
| **Wading & shore birds** | wetland/estuary food-web completeness | Wingull/Pelipper, Farfetch'd, (waders generally) | wetland productivity; **Dr. Heron's own emblem and the guild she made her name counting** (§04) |
| **Apex predators** | a food web complete enough to support a top tier | Gyarados, Sharpedo, raptor-coded birds | trophic completeness; and, via bioaccumulation, the *legacy* of contamination decades old |

The amphibian guild is the spine. It is the most sensitive, it carries the
**Poliwag line** (the keystone-indicator locked in §04), and its deformities
and disappearances are the clearest single read in the game.

### Keystone species

A species whose effect on the ecosystem is far larger than its abundance —
remove it and the system reorganizes downward (a trophic cascade). Skaldmere's
canonical keystone is **the Poliwag line in the freshwater marsh at the heart
of The Last Corridor** (LOCKED, §01/§04). Other zones have their own keystones
(a riparian engineer, a kelp-forest engineer; below). The lesson the player
internalizes: *the most important species is rarely the most impressive one.*

### Trophic cascade & mesopredator release

When a level of the food web is removed, the levels below reorganize — usually
toward **simplification**. Remove the apex and mid-level predators boom
("mesopredator release"), then over-consume the next tier down, then that tier
crashes. The player sees cascades as **skewed abundance**: a route swarming
with one mid-tier species and empty of everything above and below it is not
healthy — it is collapsing. *Abundance is not health.* This is a deliberate
inversion of the usual Pokémon instinct ("lots of Pokémon = good route").

### Bioaccumulation & biomagnification

Toxins concentrate as they move up the food web; the apex predator carries the
distilled legacy of contamination that entered the system decades and trophic
levels below it. This is why **legacy contamination outlives its source**
(Floodbasin, zone 3): the old Mereholt operation is closed, the "remediation"
is underway, and the contamination is still climbing the food web. The mask
("we already fixed that") versus the signal (deformed amphibians, sick apex).

### Edge effects, fragmentation & corridors

Cutting a habitat into pieces creates **edge** — degraded margin where the
interior conditions fail — and **isolates** the remaining populations, which
then wink out one by one even if each patch looks intact. **Corridors** are
the connective tissue that keeps isolated patches functioning as one
population. This is the ecological meaning of **The Last Corridor** (zone 8):
it is the last unbroken connection in Skaldmere, and its keystone marsh
survives *because the corridor is still whole.* Mereholt Logistics' roads and
the closing extraction frontier are narrowing it. Fragment the corridor and
even an undamaged marsh dies of isolation. **The zone's name is its ecology.**

### Range shift, phenological mismatch & the permafrost feedback

Under warming, species move upslope and poleward; timings decouple (the food
emerges before or after the consumer needs it). Cold specialists pushed
upslope eventually **run out of "up"** (Highlands, zone 5). And permafrost,
once thawed, releases stored carbon and **does not refreeze on any human
timescale** — the ground itself slumps and destabilizes (thermokarst, the
tilted "drunken forest"). Permafrost thaw is the game's purest example of
**one-way damage** (Highlands z5, Delibird Isle z6; Mereholt Permafrost, §05).

## The resilience crux (FIRMED — the intellectual heart)

This is the single most important section in the document, because it is the
scientific question the entire plot litigates.

### Regime shifts, thresholds, and hysteresis

An ecosystem absorbs disturbance while retaining its function — up to a
point. Past a **threshold**, it flips to an **alternative stable state**: a
different, usually simpler and more degraded configuration. The cruel part is
**hysteresis** — removing the disturbance does *not* flip it back. A clear
lake tipped into a turbid algae-choked state stays turbid even after the
nutrient load is cut. A kelp forest grazed into an urchin barren stays barren.
**Recovery is not the reverse of collapse.** This asymmetry — easy to break,
hard or impossible to rebuild — is the mechanical engine of the **clear-eyed
grief** ending (§02). Some of what Wren documents is already past its
threshold. No testimony brings it back.

### What "resilience" really is, and the fraud built on it

**Resilience** = a system's capacity to absorb disturbance and still retain
its essential function and identity. It is real, it is measurable, and it is
**finite and threshold-bounded.** Mereholt's greenwash commits two specific,
nameable distortions of it:

1. **Generalizing a local, temporary recovery into a universal law.** A
   population that rebounds in one protected marsh becomes "ecosystems bounce
   back" everywhere.
2. **Treating resilience as unbounded** — as if there were no threshold, as
   if a system could absorb any amount of extraction and still return.

This is exactly the seam **Dr. Heron's noble lie** opened (§04). She
overstated **Politoed recovery** in the Mahogany marshlands to win the
preserve its legal protection — inflating a fragile, real, *local* rebound
into a more robust curve than the data supported. Decades later Mereholt takes
that same inflated curve and runs both distortions on it: *Heron's own
resilience model proves ecosystems recover, therefore extraction here is
survivable, therefore these lands don't need protecting.* The lie that saved
one marsh becomes the template for de-protecting all of them — including, at
the climax knot, the Mahogany preserve the lie originally saved (§04). **The
resilience fraud is the noble lie, industrialized.**

### How the player disproves it

You cannot see a regime shift in a single visit — a snapshot can't tell a
temporary dip from a permanent flip. You can only see it across a **long
baseline**: decades of the same point, measured the same way. This is precisely
what the **Skaldmere Survey's red-cloth points** provide (§05): Ilex's
archive is the multi-decade record that lets Wren classify a place's *current*
state against its *prior* states and prove a threshold was crossed. **Ilex's
institution is what makes Wren's testimony possible** (§05). The climax's
evidentiary spine is: Heron's hand-cataloged true recovery curve at the Last
Corridor marsh + Ilex's long-baseline red-cloth data = proof that the
resilience model was inflated and that several Skaldmere systems are at or past
threshold. Honest data, finally, against the weaponized version of itself.

### Recovery & succession (when things *can* come back)

Not everything is past threshold — and the game would collapse into despair if
it were. Disturbed systems recover through **succession** (predictable stages
of recolonization) **if and only if**: the keystone survives, seed/recolonizer
sources remain connected (corridors intact), and no threshold was crossed.
Recovery is slow, partial, and never a clean rewind. This is the difference
between **stressed** and **shifted** in the survey model below — and it is what
gives the documentation work its meaning. Some places Wren records can still be
saved. Knowing which is the whole skill.

## The disturbance taxonomy (FIRMED)

Every harm in the game is one of six disturbance types, each tied to a specific
piece of the Mereholt machinery (§05) and each wearing its own mask. The
player learns to recognize them by their ecological signatures.

| Disturbance | Source (§05) | Ecological signature | The mask | Reversible? |
|-------------|--------------|----------------------|----------|-------------|
| **Contamination** | legacy Mereholt op (Floodbasin) | amphibian deformity/absence; sick apex (biomagnification) | "we're remediating that" | partial / lagged |
| **Hydrological alteration** | culverts, levees, drainage, damming (Mistwood, River & Farmland) | wetland drying; fish passage blocked; riparian loss | flood "control," culvert "upgrade" | often, if caught early |
| **Overharvest / dredging** | Mereholt Coastal (Industrial Coast) | trophic simplification (fishing down the web); benthic collapse; seabird crash | "sustainable harvest," jobs | overfishing yes; dredged benthos = regime shift |
| **Permafrost thaw / extraction** | Mereholt Permafrost (Highlands, Delibird Isle) | cold-specialist collapse; slumping ground; range shift upslope | "exploratory only"; festival cheer | **no** (carbon feedback) |
| **Fragmentation** | Mereholt Logistics roads; closing frontier (Last Corridor) | edge effects; isolated populations winking out | "improved access," "development" | only if corridor restored before isolation |
| **Eutrophication / runoff** | agricultural + industrial runoff (River & Farmland) | algal overgrowth; oxygen crash; clear→turbid regime shift | "agricultural improvement" | yes until the lake tips, then hysteresis |

## Per-zone ecology (FIRMED identities; illustrative species)

Each zone is one ecosystem under one dominant disturbance, ecologically
ordered south→north / warm→cold (§01). For each: its keystone, its indicator
read, the disturbance and its mask, and its recovery prognosis. Specific
rosters are §11; the **roles** below are firm.

### Zone 1 — Saltmarsh Coast (estuarine; tidal nursery)
- **Keystone / engineer:** the marsh grass-grazer guild that maintains marsh
  structure; the saltmarsh as nursery habitat for half the coast's fish.
- **Indicator read:** shore & wading birds (Wingull, Pelipper) and estuarine
  shellfish (Shellder, Clamperl) present and healthy = functioning nursery.
- **Disturbance:** corridor fragmentation + poaching (§01); coastal works
  creeping in from Mereholt Coastal.
- **Mask:** the tidy Foundation-crested preserve gate; "managed access."
- **Prognosis:** stressed, still recoverable. The tutorial ecosystem — where
  Wren first learns the signs read clean here so the contrast lands later.

### Zone 2 — Mistwood Forest (temperate rainforest; fog-fed)
- **Keystone:** old-growth canopy + its decomposer/disperser loop.
- **Indicator read:** lichen & fungal Pokémon (Foongus, Morelull, Shiinotic)
  — air quality and the moisture regime; seep amphibians for water.
- **Disturbance:** snares (poaching) + the shrinking culvert (hydrological
  alteration) — the locked "damage wears the mask of improvement" beat (§00).
- **Mask:** the culvert "upgrade"; the Foundation-funded, Corps-run
  snare-removal program (§05).
- **Prognosis:** reversible — if snares are removed and hydrology restored.
  This is where the player learns that some damage *can* be undone, which is
  what makes the irreversible zones later hurt.

### Zone 3 — The Floodbasin (freshwater wetland; flood-pulse)
- **Keystone:** the amphibian guild itself; the wetland's filtration function.
- **Indicator read:** amphibians (Poliwag, Wooper, Croagunk) — and here the
  read is **deformity and absence**, not just presence. The first guild to
  report poison.
- **Disturbance:** legacy contamination resurfacing from a decades-old
  Mereholt operation; flooding; agencies blaming each other (§01).
  Bioaccumulation means the apex is sick from a source long "closed."
- **Mask:** "we're remediating that" (with delays); the Foundation
  community-outreach booth (§05).
- **Prognosis:** **partial / lagged — the first zone that disproves "recovery."**
  The contamination is climbing the food web faster than the cleanup. This is
  where the player first feels that "it'll bounce back" is a lie.

### Zone 4 — River & Farmland (lotic + agricultural)
- **Keystone / ecosystem engineer:** a riparian dam-builder (Bibarel-coded) —
  whose presence *makes* wetland and whose removal *unmakes* it. The clearest
  single lesson in keystone engineering in the game.
- **Indicator read:** clean-water fish (Goldeen/Seaking, Barboach/Whiscash)
  and mayfly-coded invertebrates = good water; their loss = runoff.
- **Disturbance:** water rights, agricultural & industrial **runoff**
  (eutrophication), damming/channelization (hydrological alteration).
- **Mask:** "flood control," "agricultural improvement," irrigation works.
- **Narrative:** Harland's community's watershed; extraction's first clear
  face (§01). The ecology and the human cost meet here.
- **Prognosis:** runoff reversible *until the system tips* clear→turbid, then
  hysteresis; channelization is a regime shift. Mixed — and the player has to
  judge which parts are still savable.

### Zone 5 — The Highlands (subalpine → permafrost edge)
- **Keystone:** cold-ground specialists and alpine-meadow pollinators; the
  frozen ground itself as the substrate everything depends on.
- **Indicator read:** range shift made visible — lowland species now found too
  high; cold specialists (Snover, Sneasel, Swinub line, Cubchoo) compressed
  into a shrinking upslope band with **nowhere left to go up.**
- **Disturbance:** thawing permafrost, slope instability/slumping, Mereholt
  Permafrost "exploratory" drilling (§05).
- **Mask:** "exploratory only" signage; the Foundation-funded community center.
- **Prognosis:** **irreversible** (carbon feedback). The first visible climate
  signal; the structural setup for Delibird Isle (§01).

### Zone 6 — Delibird Isle (cold island; permafrost; festival) — social layer in §09
- **Keystone:** the cold-coast specialist community; **Delibird** as both
  species and the *give* motif (§00) — mutual aid as an ecological and social
  strategy against a hostile environment.
- **Indicator read:** Spheal line, Snorunt, Cubchoo, Delibird; and the
  *physical* indicators of thaw — thermokarst ponds, the tilted "drunken
  forest," slumping ground beneath the festival grounds.
- **Disturbance:** permafrost thaw under the festival; Mereholt Permafrost.
- **Mask:** the festival cheer itself — partly Mereholt-funded (§05); the
  Foundation-stamped storm-relief blankets.
- **Prognosis:** **irreversible.** The thesis in miniature (§01): warmth and
  generosity staged over a one-way collapse. The emotional breather that is
  secretly the whole game.

### Zone 7 — The Industrial Coast (marine; dredged; industrial fishing)
- **Keystone / engineer:** a kelp-forest engineer and the otter-coded grazer
  that protects it — the canonical real-world trophic-cascade system, made
  legible.
- **Indicator read:** forage-fish abundance, seabird colony health (Wingull/
  Pelipper crashes), and marine apex (Sharpedo, Gyarados, Lapras) presence;
  benthic invertebrates for the seafloor.
- **Disturbance:** **dredging** (benthic collapse) + **industrial
  overfishing** (fishing down the food web, trophic simplification) + port
  works. Mereholt Coastal, the operation finally made visible (§05).
- **Mask:** "sustainable harvest" certification; jobs; the catch that feeds
  Harland's community.
- **Prognosis:** overfishing reversible only if stopped before collapse;
  dredged benthos = regime shift. The economy made visible — and the place
  where the masks stop being necessary because the scale speaks for itself.

### Zone 8 — The Last Corridor (deep wilderness; the keystone marsh)
- **Keystone:** the **Poliwag line** in the small, dying freshwater marsh
  (LOCKED, §01/§04) — keystone-indicator of the marsh, and the same lineage
  as the wounded prologue Poliwag and Heron's noble lie.
- **Indicator read:** the whole-system read. The corridor is ecologically
  *complete* — every guild present — **because it is unfragmented.** That
  completeness is exactly what is now narrowing at its edges as the extraction
  frontier and Logistics roads close in. The marsh at its heart is dying not
  from a local insult but from **cumulative regional pressure plus edge
  encroachment** — the canary for all of Skaldmere.
- **Disturbance:** fragmentation (the corridor narrowing) + the summed weight
  of every upstream disturbance.
- **Mask:** none. There is no Mereholt presence here (§05) — the wilderness is
  too raw to extract economically *yet,* which is why Heron could slip away to
  die here. **The absence of the mask is the point.**
- **Prognosis:** the climax's open question. It may already be past threshold.
  Heron is hand-cataloging the *true* recovery curve — the honest version of
  the number her lie inflated — and Ilex's red-cloth baselines will prove
  whether the line has crossed. **Clear-eyed grief** (§02): Wren documents it,
  testifies, and the testimony does not guarantee the marsh comes back.

## The survey-state model (FIRMED — bridge to §10/§11)

This is where the ecology becomes the **encounter table.** A route is not a
fixed list of spawns; it has an **ecological state**, and its encounter table,
species condition, and abundance distribution are *functions of that state.*
The player's core action — the **transect/point survey** — is reading that
state against a baseline (the red-cloth point; §05). §06 defines the four
states and their signatures; §10 defines the survey *mechanic*; §11 places the
state-dependent *tables*.

| State | Keystone | Indicator guilds | Abundance shape | Reverts? |
|-------|----------|------------------|-----------------|----------|
| **Reference** (healthy) | present, breeding | full guild, good condition | balanced pyramid, apex present | n/a |
| **Stressed** | present, strained | sensitive indicators thinning / poor condition | mildly skewed; apex thinning | **yes**, recovers if relieved |
| **Collapsing** | failing | sensitive guild gone; deformities; contamination signs | mesopredator release — one tier swarms, others empty | only if caught *now* |
| **Shifted** (post-threshold) | absent | original community gone; generalists/invasives/disturbance species dominate | new, simpler equilibrium | **no** (hysteresis) |

Design consequences:
- **Abundance is a trap.** A "Collapsing" route can be *swarming* (mesopredator
  release) and a "Reference" route comparatively quiet. The game teaches the
  player to distrust the old "lots of spawns = good" reflex (§ Trophic cascade).
- **The same coordinates read differently across the game.** A point the
  player surveys as "Stressed" early can be re-surveyed later as "Collapsing"
  or "Shifted" — the world degrades on the clock of the plot, and the
  red-cloth baseline is how the player *proves* it moved.
- **Certifications map to states** (§01's "competencies, not type-gyms"):
  *survey* = read the state; *restraint* = don't intervene in a Stressed
  system that will recover on its own; *response* = act in a Collapsing one
  while there's still time; *remediation* = the slow succession work that
  *might* pull a system back from the edge. The ladder is a curriculum in
  reading and responding to these four states.

## Motif species (FIRMED — ties to canon)

The recurring species carry the game's symbols (§00):

- **Smeargle = record.** The act of surveying *is* the Smeargle act — paint
  what you see, keep the eye open. Sketch (Wren's lost childhood Smeargle,
  §04) is the personal root of the survey instinct; the notebook is Sketch's
  eye and Heron's eye, doubled. The whole ecology bible is, in-world, the kind
  of thing a Smeargle-keeper produces.
- **Delibird = give.** Mutual aid as survival strategy — ecological
  (cooperation in a harsh environment) and social (the gift economy of zone 6,
  §09). The counter-thesis to extraction: *take* vs. *give.*
- **The wading birds = Dr. Heron.** Her name, her emblem, and the indicator
  guild she built her reputation counting by hand (§04). Every heron, egret,
  or wader Wren logs is a quiet invocation of her — and of the honest field
  work the lie betrayed.
- **The Poliwag line = the whole knot.** Keystone, indicator, the prologue
  wound, the noble lie's species, and the dying marsh's last tenant — one
  lineage carrying the prologue, the betrayal, and the climax (§04). When the
  player finally understands the ecology, they understand why the first frog
  on the first bridge was the entire game.
- **Red cloth = the baseline.** Not a species but the Survey's measurement
  marker (§05) — the physical thing that makes a regime shift *visible* and a
  vanished community *memorialized.* The ecology is only legible because
  someone tied cloth at the same point for forty years.

## How the ecology teaches itself (FIRMED — ties to §04 mechanic)

The player is not lectured. The ecology is learned the way Wren learns it:

- **The notebook's marginalia decode into ecology** (§04). A margin note that
  reads as a squiggle in Act I resolves, as competence grows, into a precise
  field cue — an indicator-guild key, a survey method, a keystone
  identification. Reading the notebook deeper *is* learning to read the land.
  Heron teaches the ecology posthumously-but-not, on Wren's schedule.
- **The signs contradict the signposts.** Each zone pairs a Foundation/Council
  "improvement" claim with an ecological signal that refutes it. The player
  learns by catching the contradiction — the mask coming off is the lesson.
- **The states are felt before they are named.** The player surveys
  "Reference" Saltmarsh before they have the word for it, so that "Collapsing"
  Floodbasin lands in the body before it lands in the vocabulary.
