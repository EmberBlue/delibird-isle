## Opening scroll (canonical, verbatim)

> "In an age of unstable climates and shrinking wildlands, the bond between
> people and Pokémon has changed. Some work to restore balance. Others seek
> control."
>
> "You've just arrived from Johto, leaving behind your old home and Pokémon.
> You carry only a bag, a notebook… and a memory."

*Fade in from a ferry horn. The player (Wren — §04) steps off at a small,
cold coastal town with luggage and a letter. No fanfare.*

## Scene 1 — Dock / station arrival

Grey water, wind-rippled waves, a fading ferry horn; boots on damp wood. The
town is modest — a few homes, a general store, a pier-side shelter. Not snowy;
cold *coastal* air — salt, fog, gull cries. An NPC in a heavy coat points the
way. No welcome banner. **A routine arrival that feels slightly watched, like
the town is holding its breath.**

Player control begins; residents are subtly evasive, not openly hostile.

**Key NPC beats (recommended):**
- A shopkeeper mentions "strange weather inland" like an everyday nuisance.
- A neighbor asks if you're **"the one the scientist wrote about,"** then
  quickly changes the subject.
  - *What we now know:* Dr. Heron wrote *about* Wren — in an essay, a journal
    acknowledgement, the margins of letters (§04). This is the locals
    recognizing the protégé. It plays warmer and sadder once you know she's
    the one in exile at the end.
- A fisher at the pier says the currents "have been wrong for weeks."

## Scene 2 — The mentor's letter

At the lodging, Wren reads the letter. Warm, purposeful, practical — an
invitation to learn, to help, to **witness.** It references work on *habitat
resilience* and *migration corridors* and hints at something politically
sensitive found.

**Letter tone (canonical lines, verbatim):**

> "You don't need to be brilliant. You need to be honest."
>
> "If the data is real, people will try to bury it. Not because they're
> evil — because it threatens what they've built."
>
> "Meet me at the lab in the morning. Come early. Bring curiosity. Bring
> boots."

- *What we now know:* every word is true and every word is a trap she walked
  into first. "You need to be honest" is written by someone who once *wasn't*
  — the noble lie (§04) — and who is, as she writes this, being leashed by it.
  The notebook Wren carries is hers, annotated in hopeful layers before the
  silence (§04, the marginalia mechanic). She means the advice. It costs her
  everything to have failed it once.

## Scene 3 — The erased lab (destroyed with precision)

Next morning: the lab door is damaged but **not blown open.** Inside — shelves
emptied, drives removed, sample jars gone, a desk lamp on its side. Someone
**carefully took what mattered, then staged the mess.** An erasure, not a
burglary. A local official appears and dismisses it, bureaucratic and
practiced:

> **Official:** "No signs of forced entry beyond the latch. Could be
> vandalism. We've filed it. Not much else we can do."

A resident won't meet your eyes. Another whispers: **"Don't make noise about
it."**

- *What we now know:* the erasure was **staged by Mereholt's machinery** to
  sell the story that Dr. Heron was *silenced for getting too close* (§00,
  §01). The whole front half of the game runs on that false read. She is not
  dead and not buried — she is **collaborating under coercion** and will be
  found alive in zone 8 (§04). The official's practiced calm is the Council's
  capture (§05) in miniature, two minutes into the game.

## Scene 4 — The only bridge out *(the load-bearing scene)*

The island town connects to the mainland by one long bridge — the first gate
and the first set-piece. Wren crosses with **no Pokémon yet** (non-battle
tutorial state). Wind over the rail, water surging beneath.

Halfway across: a cluster of **Poliwag** gathered at the lower embankment and
the stream mouth, **panicked. Not blocking for comedy — stuck.** Something
upstream drove them here. Wren approaches; they flinch but don't flee, eyes
wide. The camera lingers until the player understands: **these are animals
under pressure.**

- *What we now know — this is the most important silent payload in the game.*
  The **Poliwag line** is the species Dr. Heron's noble lie once protected
  (the falsified Politoed recovery, §04), the keystone-indicator of the dying
  marsh at the climax (§06), and the lineage the concern's greenwash now leans
  on. **The first creatures the player ever sees in distress are the exact
  thread that ties the prologue to the confession.** The player cannot know
  this yet. The bridge plays completely differently on a second read (§04).
  Build it so the staging *rewards* that re-read — quiet, lingering, no
  dialogue rushing it.

## Scene 5 — Poacher ambush (functional, not cartoonish)

A Poacher and two thugs step from under the bridge approach — nets, crates, a
crude scanner. **Functional, not cartoonish.**

> **Poacher:** "Perfect. They ran right where we need them. Don't touch
> anything, kid. You'll spook the stock."
>
> **Player:** "…Stock?"
>
> **Poacher:** "You heard me."

Wren has no Pokémon to fight. The Poacher begins capture attempts; Poliwag
scatter helplessly. **The scene makes the player feel small and angry — but
not powerless forever.**

## Scene 6 — Ranger intervention (Hale)

A sharp whistle. **Hale** steps onto the bridge approach — calm posture, no
swagger — and throws a Poké Ball with practiced, minimal movement.

> **Hale:** "Step away from the water."
>
> **Poacher:** "This isn't your jurisdiction."
>
> **Hale:** "It's a living corridor. That's enough."

Battle: **Hale (NPC) vs. the Poacher** — the player watches. The poacher
retreats, frustrated, **not defeated in ideology**, and drops the line meant
to haunt:

> **Poacher:** "You think you're early? You're late."

Hale kneels by the stream, watching the Poliwag slip upstream in a tight,
trembling group, and assesses the player **like a tool, not a hero:**

> **Hale:** "This region doesn't need heroes. It needs witnesses."
>
> **Hale:** "If you want answers, come south. There's a station across the
> bridge. You can learn to do more than stare."

- *What we now know:* "You're late" is literal — the decline is decades deep
  (the long-baseline regime shifts of §06); Wren is arriving at the end of a
  process, not the start of one. And Hale's "witnesses, not heroes" is the
  **player-arc thesis** (§00: witness → provisional actor → accountable
  participant) and the game's refusal of the chosen-one frame, stated aloud in
  the first ten minutes by a man who is himself leashed by Mereholt funding he
  hasn't mentioned (§05).

**Transition:** Hale escorts Wren south to the first mainland ranger station.
**Enrollment happens there, not here** (§08).
