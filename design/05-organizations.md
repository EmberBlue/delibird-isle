# 05 — Organizations

> Status: **FIRMED** (the three load-bearing entities — Mereholt, the
> Council, the Climate Corps — and the lever-holder are locked; secondary
> orgs and named officers below are revisable). The antagonist is an
> **economy**, not a villain (§02). This section gives that economy real
> machinery: the firm, the people who sign the papers, the bureaus they
> capture, and the agencies they leash.

## Reading guide

Three lessons sit behind this section, in order of weight:

1. **Everyone is leashed.** Harland is leashed by his paycheck. Dr. Heron is
   leashed by an old secret and a grant cycle. The Foundation officer who
   *holds her leash* is also leashed by the board. The Climate Corps is
   leashed by its funding mix. The pattern is the thesis.
2. **The face of the system is always pleasant.** Mereholt's preserve signs
   are clean. The Foundation's offices are bright. The officer who delivers
   the lever does so with real warmth. Menace is in spreadsheets, not faces.
3. **Tracing the same name across zones is the discovery.** The Mereholt
   logo on a preserve gate in zone 1 is the same logo on a fishing fleet
   hull in zone 7 is the same logo on Dr. Heron's grant letterhead. The
   player learns to *read the corporate kerning* the way they learn to
   read tracks.

## Mereholt (LOCKED): the extraction concern

### Public face: **The Mereholt Foundation**

A conservation-grants and community-stewardship arm. What the public sees:

- Funds Dr. Heron's lab, has for two decades. Her funding letters are signed
  here.
- Sponsors the Climate Corps's ranger gear, certain field stations, and a
  slice of certification training. Logos on hat patches and equipment cases.
- Underwrites preserve signage across Skaldmere — the green-and-grey crest
  on the gate at every protected area Wren passes through in zones 1–6.
- Underwrites parts of the Delibird Isle festival (zone 6) — banners,
  storm-relief blankets.
- Publishes a glossy annual report titled *Stewardship.*

**Chair:** a respected, recently-retired Climate Corps district director —
*name TBD;* see Open. A reputation borrowed for legitimacy. He believes,
mostly, that he is doing late-career good. He is wrong, mostly. He is also
leashed — by a comfortable pension and by his own self-image as a man who
finally figured out how to "work from the inside."

### Real entity: **Mereholt Resources Ltd**

The holding company. What the player learns to see by zone 7:

- **Mereholt Coastal** — industrial fishing fleet, port works, dredging.
  The hulls Wren sees at the Industrial Coast (zone 7).
- **Mereholt Permafrost** — exploratory drilling and extraction in the
  Highlands (zone 5) and on Delibird Isle (zone 6).
- **Mereholt Logistics** — shipping, freight, the trucks Wren has been
  passing on every road since the Saltmarsh.
- Wholly owns the Mereholt Foundation. The Foundation is a 100% subsidiary
  with a separate board of trustees, structured for grant-making
  tax-efficiency and reputational laundering.

**Chair & CEO:** **Cordelia Brooke.** Second-generation leadership; her
father ran the regional firm that Mereholt absorbed, and she has internalized
the family creed that *Mereholt **is** the regional economy.* Mid-fifties,
polished, decent in her own self-conception, lethal in her policy
discipline. The player's final-act adversary battle (§02) is Cordelia in a
boardroom, not in a cave. She uses plausible, locally-appropriate Pokémon
(no cartoon roster); her team is curated more for boardroom-photo neutrality
than for combat advantage.

She is **also leashed** — by shareholders, by long-term debt covenants tied
to extraction throughput, by a board that would replace her in a week if
quarterly figures faltered. She knows this. She mentions it, once, calmly,
during the confrontation. It is not an excuse and she does not offer it as
one.

## The lever-holder (LOCKED): Hollis Aune

**Hollis Aune,** Conservation Program Officer, the Mereholt Foundation. Late
thirties. Formerly Dr. Heron's graduate student, in the wetlands-resilience
years. Went into "conservation finance" out of school, believing it was the
way to make a real difference — that you could not save habitats from
outside the rooms where money is decided, you had to be inside those rooms.
She believed this in good faith. She has never quite stopped believing it.

She signs Dr. Heron's funding letters every year. She visits when she can.
She speaks of "Dr. H" with the affection of someone who still keeps her
teacher's marginalia in her own field copies. She is the warm face the lever
wears.

**She is the third leg of the rhyme.** Harland is leashed by his paycheck.
Dr. Heron is leashed by an old lie and a grant. **Hollis is leashed by the
Mereholt board**, who hold a complete record of every conversation she has
had with Dr. Heron — every favor, every renewed grant, every warm visit —
and have made it clear, gently, that *she* is the one who delivered the
lever, that *she* is the chain of custody, that her career and her name
will be the second casualty if Mereholt's interests are not preserved.

She knows. She has known for at least three years. She has been delivering
the lever to Dr. Heron in increasingly euphemistic language. She has been
delivering an identical lever, in identical language, to herself.

### What this gives the climax

- **Wren meets Hollis** before the climax — likely zone 7, the Industrial
  Coast, in a polite Foundation office above the docks. Hollis offers tea
  and explains, kindly, that Dr. Heron's lab is in a "delicate funding
  cycle" and that Wren's surveys, if published indiscriminately, could
  jeopardize *Dr. Heron's life's work.* It will be the kindest, most
  carefully worded threat in the game. Wren will not know it is a threat
  until much later.
- **At the climax,** Wren faces a third collateral choice on top of Dr.
  Heron and the Mahogany preserve: *expose Hollis too, or spare her
  knowing she'll be next in the same chair.* The game offers no easy
  answer. The clear-eyed grief is wider for the choice.

## The Council & Councilman Dorsey (LOCKED at the agency level)

### Skaldmere Regional Council

The regional governing body. Modeled on real-world rural-coastal regional
councils: elected, under-resourced, deeply dependent on a small number of
major employers. The Council is not corrupt in the cartoon sense — it is
**captured** in the regulatory-economics sense. The dominant employer is
Mereholt. The Council's career officers know which permits cannot be denied
without economic retaliation, and they shape their own recommendations
accordingly. Most of them sleep fine.

### Councilman Dorsey

**Commissioner of Resource Development and Public-Private Partnerships,**
Skaldmere Regional Council. Specifically the role that brokers the
permitting interface between Mereholt and the Council. Dorsey is the
political face of every "improvement" that wears a mask of harm.

- **Revolving door.** Was previously a senior advisor at the Mereholt
  Foundation (3 years), before standing for Council. Will likely return to
  Mereholt — or to a Mereholt-adjacent consultancy — after his Council
  term. Everyone knows this. No one finds it remarkable. This is how the
  ladder works in Skaldmere.
- **He is sincere.** Genuinely believes the region cannot afford an
  adversarial relationship with its largest employer. Frames the Climate
  Corps as outsiders not because he is lying — because he half-believes
  it. The most dangerous antagonists in this game believe themselves.
- **Plausible roster.** Dorsey's Pokémon are political-image-coded: a
  Slowking (sage-old-statesman), a Pidgeot (visible from a distance, never
  on the ground with anyone). Curated for press photos.
- **What he does to the player.** Manages perception of every event
  zones 3–6. Personally appears to dismiss the Poisoned Waters event
  (Chapter 1, §00). Steps aside at zone 7 — by then the economy speaks for
  itself and Dorsey doesn't need to.

## The Climate Corps (LOCKED at the agency level)

### Institutional shape

The **Skaldmere Climate Corps** is a regional ranger agency under the
Council's environmental portfolio. Professional, not volunteer. Rangers
hold certified credentials, not badges; certifications stack into a public
career ladder. The Corps's authority is real but limited: they can survey,
document, restrain, remove snares, issue citations, and (rarely) seize
evidence. They cannot block a permit. They cannot revoke a license. *They
cannot punch a paycheck.*

- **Chain of command.** Hale (§04) is the player's direct field contact, a
  district station chief. His superior — the Regional Director — is mostly
  off-screen and is a careful political animal. The certification authority
  sits at the Director's office; certifications are issued there, not at
  field stations. Field stations *prepare* you; the office *credentials* you.
- **The Surveyors** (below) are a sister agency, not a sub-unit. The two
  agencies cooperate in the field but report separately and are funded
  separately.

### Funding (LOCKED): partially Mereholt-funded

Roughly two-thirds of the Climate Corps's operating budget comes from
regional appropriations (clean, public). The remaining third comes from
**Mereholt Foundation conservation grants**, channeled through
public-private partnership instruments brokered by Dorsey's office. The
Foundation slice covers: ranger gear, the certification training program,
two of the field stations Wren will visit, and the Corps's regional
research budget.

This is not a fringe slice. It is the difference between a Corps that runs
twelve field stations and a Corps that runs eight. The Director knows it.
Hale knows it. **The Corps is leashed too** — not as tightly as Heron, not
as personally as Hollis, but leashed.

- **What this does to Hale.** His stoicism is not peace. *"We don't 'win.'
  We reduce harm."* takes on a second meaning: he is also describing what
  the Foundation funding does to the Corps. He has spent his career
  figuring out what real work can be done inside an institution that
  cannot afford to be fully honest about its sponsors. He has not solved
  it. He is the closest thing to a guide in this game because he stopped
  pretending the leash could be removed and started figuring out how much
  could still be done with it on.
- **What this does to certifications.** The certification ladder is a real
  credential, recognized inter-regionally. It is also, partially, a
  Mereholt-funded credential. The player who earns the final certification
  will have, on paper, a credential underwritten by the firm whose
  greenwash they are about to expose. This is acknowledged but never
  resolved. The credential is not invalid. It is also not innocent.

## The Skaldmere Survey (FIRMED at agency level)

A small, professional, semi-academic mapping agency. Not part of the
Climate Corps. Maps environmental change *over time* — long-baseline
monitoring, soil-core archives, repeat photography, hydrological logs. Their
work is too slow and too obscure to weaponize, which is also why they are
chronically underfunded: honest mapping does not generate press releases.

**Ilex** (§04) is a Surveyor.

- **The red cloth motif.** Surveyors tie a strip of red cloth at every
  measurement point — a marker, originally functional (a fast way to find
  the point again next season). Over years, repeated tying at the same
  point makes the cloth a **memorial register.** A point that has been
  tied for forty years and is now an empty footprint where a wetland used
  to be wears decades of cloth, layered, faded, knotted, an unintentional
  shrine. Ilex carries fresh red cloth as standard equipment. The player
  will see this cloth at multiple points before they understand what it is.
- **Funding.** Tiny public appropriation; some university partnerships;
  *not* Mereholt-touched. The Survey is the one agency in this section
  that is meaningfully *unleashed.* It survives this by being too small
  and too slow to be worth capturing. Ilex's unsettling quiet comes from
  this — they are the one professional in the region who can afford to
  say what they actually see.
- **What the Survey gives Wren.** Late-game, when Wren needs to prove a
  multi-decade pattern (the resilience-model fraud, the keystone-marsh
  decline), they will need Survey data — and Ilex's red-cloth points
  become the spine of the climax's evidentiary case. **Ilex's institution
  is what makes Wren's testimony possible.**

## Smaller stakeholders (sketched, not firmed)

- **The fishing community** — not a formal union but a real social
  collective. Centered on Harland (§04). Politically aligned with Mereholt
  Coastal because Mereholt Coastal is who buys their catch and underwrites
  their boats. *They are not the enemy. They are the cost.*
- **Highland & Delibird Isle homesteaders** — small communities directly
  affected by permafrost thaw. Less captured by Mereholt because they have
  less to lose to it; more politically alienated; more likely to be open to
  Wren in a way Harland's community is not.
- **The Surveyors' adjacent academy** — a small university partnership in
  the Highlands. Source of Ilex's training and of some Survey funding.
  Probably the place Hollis Aune did her graduate work under Dr. Heron.
  This last point is not coincidence. Skaldmere is small.
