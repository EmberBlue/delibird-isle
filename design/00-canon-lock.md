## Premise

In an age of unstable climates and shrinking wildlands, the bond between people
and Pokémon has changed. Some work to restore balance. Others seek control.
The player arrives from Johto — leaving behind their old home and Pokémon —
carrying only a bag, a notebook, and a memory. They are not a chosen hero.
They become a **witness**, then a **provisional actor**, then an **accountable
participant** in a region under quiet ecological and institutional stress.

## Locked decisions

### Identity
- **Title:** Pokémon Anthropocene.
- **Region type:** large, island-based region with diverse biomes and
  architecture tied to ecology and history.
- **Delibird Isle:** a *location within* the game — a festive, chilly island
  whose cheerful surface hides ecological stress (thawing permafrost). Not the
  whole game.
- **Mascots:** Delibird + Smeargle. They embody the core loop —
  **Smeargle = document/observe/record** (the player's notebook made flesh);
  **Delibird = deliver/aid/steward** (help given during storms). Together:
  *observe → respond → document → act.*

### Tone
- Grounded, mature, slightly heavier than mainline Pokémon. "Quiet dread"
  escalating into responsibility, with warmth and hope underneath.
- Cheerful + eerie contrast is a deliberate device, not a tonal slip.

### Player & progression
- Player arc: **witness → provisional actor → accountable participant.** Never
  a chosen-one; the world exists with or without them.
- **No gyms.** Progression is via **Ranger Certifications** — credentials, not
  trophies. Earned through ecological competence (surveys, restraint, response).
- Large full region; many certifications across many biomes.

### Antagonism
- Systemic, bureaucratic, ecological — **never a cartoon villain team.**
- No stereotyped "evil-team Pokémon" rosters. Opponents use plausible,
  opportunistic, locally-appropriate Pokémon.
- Pressure comes from: **Industrialists** (systemic extraction, not
  supervillains), manipulated locals, poachers-as-laborers, and institutional
  inertia. "Damage often wears the mask of improvement."

### Pokémon & gameplay
- **Roster:** Generations 1–3 only, plus related Gen 4 continuations / baby
  forms / evolutions. (Delibird & Smeargle are Gen 2 — both qualify.)
- **Full Pokémon game:** robust catching, breeding, and battling. The mature
  framing lives in the narrative and key story beats, not in gutting the loop.
- **Pokémon as ecological indicators.** Encounter placement follows ecological
  logic — no route filler, no random stuffing.
- **Starters choose you.** No menu. The player walks among "edge species" that
  tolerate human proximity, and one recognizes them through ecological fit.
  Canonical set: Shroomish, Seedot, Lotad, Carvanha, Numel, Electrike, Doduo.

### Engine
- Built on **pokeemerald-expansion** (already checked out in this repo).

### Story shape (the macro arc)
- **No legendaries, no regional myth.** Nothing swoops in to fix things. The
  stakes stay human and ecological. The "climax Pokémon" problem is solved by
  making the finale a *reckoning of testimony*, not a legendary battle.
- **The antagonist is an economy, not a person:** large-scale resource
  extraction (mining / drilling / industrial fishing) that hollows the region.
  Short-term jobs and survival vs. long-term collapse. Harland's community is
  caught in the middle; you cannot punch a paycheck.
- **The mentor is complicit / compromised.** Their ecological research
  ("habitat resilience," "migration corridors") was co-opted into the
  *greenwash* that legitimizes the extraction. The player believes they are
  uncovering a silencing; they are actually uncovering a collaboration. The lab
  "erasure" was staged. **Motive: coerced** — leashed by personal cost /
  leverage, not greed or defeatism. Partly a victim; the most *forgivable*
  motive, which sharpens the grief (specific lever: OPEN — see §04).
- **The notebook is the mentor's.** Every survey the player runs completes the
  dataset that can expose the extraction *or* further legitimize it. Honesty is
  both the weapon and the question.
- **The climax** is the player's accumulated honest record forcing a reckoning;
  the emotional "final boss" is the mentor. Resolution is bittersweet — harm
  reduced, not a clean victory (per Hale: "We don't win. We reduce harm").
- **Mascot integration:** Delibird & Smeargle are **recurring motifs, not plot
  devices.** Smeargle = recording / witnessing (surveyors' companions, murals,
  the act of sketching the world). Delibird = giving (Delibird Isle's festival
  and delivery culture, storm-relief). They are the region's emotional
  vocabulary — *giving vs. taking, honest record vs. greenwash* — woven into
  texture, not bolted to quests. NB: the **mentor's field notebook** is a
  separate, load-bearing *object* (honesty made physical), not a mascot.

### Unifying insight (the thesis the cast rhymes on)
- The extraction economy's real weapon is **leverage over the vulnerable** —
  the same gun at two heads: Harland's livelihood, the mentor's secret. No one
  is evil; everyone is leashed. The opposition reduces harm and keeps an honest
  record; it never "wins."

## Anti-rules (never do)

- No chosen-one / prophecy framing.
- No cartoon villain organization or stereotyped evil-team Pokémon.
- No constant spectacle or apocalyptic escalation.
- No gym-badge power-fantasy formula.
- No exposition dumps or melodrama; dialogue implies more than it states.
- No ecological placement without logic.

  corresponded with the mentor for years; carries the mentor's invitation, a
  notebook, and the memory of **Sketch**, their childhood Smeargle, who died in
  the wetlands south of Goldenrod (small habitat death; Wren missed the signs).
  Arrives with no Pokémon by choice. Fully defined character; player picks
  pronouns only. Full detail: §04.

- **Rin** — Ranger, practical, tired, observant.
- **Ilex** — Surveyor: maps change and timelines, not solutions. Quietly
  unsettling and intelligent. Associated with a recurring red-cloth symbol.
- **The Mentor** — the scientist who wrote the player's invitation letter;

- **Captain Harland** — proud, frustrated fisher; *misled*, not evil.
- **Councilman Dorsey** — smooth manipulator; weaponizes blame and "relief."
- **Industrialists** — systemic extraction pressure; the engine of the plot.

## Established narrative canon (locked beats)

Full long-form prose lives in §07 (Prologue) and §08 (Chapter 1). The locked
skeleton:

### Prologue — Arrival at the Edge of Change
1. **Dock arrival** — grey water, cold coastal town, the feeling of being
   watched. No fanfare.
2. **The mentor's letter** — warm, practical. "You don't need to be brilliant.
   You need to be honest." "If the data is real, people will try to bury it."
3. **The erased lab** — emptied with precision; an erasure staged as a
   burglary. An official dismisses it; residents look away.
4. **The only bridge out** — panicked, stuck Poliwag driven downstream.
5. **Poacher ambush** — functional, not cartoonish. "They ran right where we
   need them." "…Stock?" "You think you're early? You're late."
6. **Ranger Hale intervenes** — "It's a living corridor. That's enough." Sends
   the player south to the first station.

### Chapter 1 — Forest Ranger Certification & the Mentor's Legacy
1. **First station** (mainland, south of the bridge) — Hale explains the
   Climate Corps in plain terms.
2. **Partner selection (Act IV, "The Ones That Wait")** — the holding meadow;
   edge species; the player walks, observes, and is *recognized* (e.g. Doduo).
   "You don't pick. And they don't obey."
3. **Certification loop** — remove snares, run a habitat survey, take a water
   sample, answer a distress call (first real battle). The badge is a credential.
4. **Wetlands edge** — first poacher-network clues.
5. **Harland** introduced; points the player toward **Dorsey**.
6. **The Poisoned Waters event** — first public-perception crisis; evidence
   implicates Industrialist-linked movement.
7. **Confrontation** — an enforcer (not Harland). "Your mentor tried. Look how
   that went." Dorsey slips away; Harland steps back, shaken.
8. **First assignment (Act V, "The Corridor That Shrank")** — a low-priority
   culvert audit; an orange-vest worker offers "grates next month." The lesson:
   **damage wears the mask of improvement.** The player documents, withholds
   consent, and does *not* intervene physically.
