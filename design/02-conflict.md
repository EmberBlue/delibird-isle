> not a villain. There is no legendary, no chosen one, and no clean win.

## The core engine: extraction

Large-scale resource extraction (mining / drilling / industrial fishing) is
quietly hollowing the region. It is not run by cartoon villains — it is run by
people inside a system of incentives:

- **Industrialists — Mereholt** (LOCKED, §05). Public face: the Mereholt
  Foundation (conservation grants, preserve signage, Foundation funds Dr.
  Heron's lab and a third of the Climate Corps's budget). Real entity:
  Mereholt Resources Ltd, with Coastal / Permafrost / Logistics subsidiaries.
  CEO **Cordelia Brooke** — polished, lawful-on-paper, genuinely believes
  Mereholt **is** the regional economy. Power is *legitimacy*, not menace.
- **Councilman Dorsey** — Commissioner of Resource Development and
  Public-Private Partnerships, Skaldmere Regional Council (§05). Sells
  "relief" and "development," weaponizes blame, frames the Climate Corps as
  outsiders. The Council is captured, not corrupt — it cannot afford to deny
  Mereholt anything that affects throughput.
- **Captain Harland** — a proud fisher whose livelihood depends on the very
  extraction economy. Misled, not evil. The human cost made personal. *You
  cannot punch a paycheck.*
- **Poachers / enforcers** — labor, not masterminds. Opportunistic, plausible
  Pokémon. Cynics, not zealots: "The land's already dead. We're just the first
  to admit it."

The opposition (Climate Corps / Rangers / Surveyors) cannot "defeat" this. They
reduce harm, keep corridors open, and **document**.

## The throughline: the mentor's collaboration

The emotional spine. The player believes they are uncovering a **silencing**;
they are actually uncovering a **collaboration**.

- The mentor's research — *"habitat resilience," "migration corridors"* (the
  prologue letter's own words) — was **co-opted into the greenwash** that
  legitimizes the extraction: "our models show the ecosystem is resilient, so
  this is sustainable."
- The lab "erasure" was **staged** — not a ransacking. It reads as a murder/
  silencing for most of the game; the reveal is that the mentor walked away.
- **The notebook the player carries is the mentor's** — a load-bearing *object*,
  honesty made physical (not a mascot/mechanic gimmick). Every survey extends
  the dataset that can *expose* the extraction **or** *further legitimize* it.
  The player's honesty is both the weapon and the open question.
- **Mentor's motive: coerced** (§04). Not greed, not defeatism — Dr. Heron was
  **leashed by a buried dishonesty + lab funding** (§04). Partly a victim; the
  most forgivable motive, which is exactly why it hurts. **The lever-holder
  is Hollis Aune** — Dr. Heron's former graduate student, now Mereholt
  Foundation Conservation Program Officer, herself leashed by the Mereholt
  board (§05). This makes the mentor *rhyme with Harland* and *with Hollis* —
  three people squeezed by the same economy at three different layers.

## Escalation (mapped to §01)

- Z3–4: extraction's effects appear as "accidents" and "improvements" (poisoned
  backwater, a dam, grates that exclude wildlife). Dorsey manages perception.
- Z5–6: the climate signal becomes undeniable (permafrost thaw); Delibird Isle
  shows a community absorbing the cost with mutual aid.
- Z7: the extraction operation itself — the economy made physical and visible.
- Z8: the last intact corridor; what everyone is actually fighting over.

## The climax: a reckoning of testimony (no legendary, single authored ending)

The finale is **not** a battle for a box legend, and it does **not** branch. One
strong, crafted, bittersweet ending. It is the player's accumulated honest
record forcing a public/regulatory reckoning that the extraction expansion
depends on. The emotional "final boss" is the **mentor**, arguing their case
(now from coercion, not greed): *"They had me. I kept it slow. Without me it
would have been worse. You think honesty fixes anything?"* The player's
documentation drives the outcome narratively, but converges on one authored
landing — not a menu of endings.

Battles still exist (the head of the concern; the mentor) — grounded people with
plausible teams, never cartoon villains.

**Emotional note: clear-eyed grief** (Hale: "We don't win. We reduce harm").
Bitter, cold, true. The expansion is only *delayed*; the mentor is gone or
disgraced; the extraction continues, constrained. To expose the greenwash the
player tells the whole truth — which **destroys the teacher who taught them
honesty**, and (per the climax knot, §04) may un-protect the very preserve the
mentor's old lie once saved. Honesty with real collateral; the game refuses to
say it was simply right. The notebook becomes the player's own — a first true
entry, chosen knowing exactly what it cost.
