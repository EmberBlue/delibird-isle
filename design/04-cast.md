# 04 — Cast Bible

> The emotional core. **No one here is evil; everyone is leashed.** The
> extraction economy coerces through leverage over the vulnerable.

## The Mentor (your teacher; the one who wrote the letter)

- Respected regional ecologist. Invited the player to *witness* and *be honest*
  (prologue letter, §07). The player carries their **field notebook**.
- **Truth:** not silenced — **coerced into collaboration.** Their research on
  *habitat resilience* and *migration corridors* was turned into the greenwash
  that legitimizes the extraction. The lab "erasure" was **staged**.
- **Motive: coerced / personal cost (LOCKED).** Not greed, not defeatism. They
  were leashed by something held over them. This makes them *partly a victim* —
  the most forgivable motive — which is exactly why the reveal grieves rather
  than enrages. They **rhyme with Harland**: both squeezed by the same economy.

### The three-layer reveal (structure)
1. **Silencing** — the player (and world) assume the mentor was buried for
   getting too close. Most of the game runs on this belief.
2. **Collaboration** — the player discovers the mentor's science is *inside* the
   greenwash. Betrayal.
3. **Coercion** — the final layer: they didn't sell out, they were *forced*.
   Grief replaces anger. There is no clean villain to hate, no clean teacher to
   mourn.

### The lever (LOCKED): a buried dishonesty + money (doubled leash)
The concern holds **both** halves and needs no thugs — just an accountant and a
filing cabinet:
- **The secret:** the mentor once committed a buried dishonesty (exact shape
  OPEN, below). Exposure discredits not just them but the entire conservation
  movement built on their name.
- **The money:** the concern funds the mentor's lab / life's work. Pull it and
  everything (and everyone employed) dies.
- **The threat:** *"We know what you did, and we fund what you built. Give us the
  resilience models, or both die together."* This is the **same economic lever
  as on Harland** — survival — which makes the thesis airtight.

### The buried dishonesty (LOCKED): the noble lie
Early in their career the mentor **fudged data to *protect* a habitat** —
overstated a species' recovery to secure a legal preserve. Born of love for the
land. Decades later the concern weaponizes it to *destroy* the land. This
complicates "be honest" perfectly: the letter the player carries is at once a
**confession, a warning, and the thing that dooms them.** The crusader's one
lie, told to save a place, becomes the weapon that ruins it.

### The climax knot (LOCKED): honesty has collateral
To expose the greenwash, the player **must also expose the mentor's lie** —
destroying the teacher who taught them honesty, **and de-protecting the preserve
the lie once saved** (its legal protection rested on the fudged recovery data).
Truth-telling has maximum collateral. The player chooses it anyway; the game
refuses to say it was simply right. This is the engine of the clear-eyed-grief
ending (§02).

## The Climate Corps (your colleagues; grounded professionals)

- **Hale** — Ranger; calm competence, quiet authority, no swagger. The closest
  thing to a guide. *"This region doesn't need heroes. It needs witnesses."*
  *"We don't 'win.' We reduce harm."* *"Get attached to results, not praise."*
- **Rin** — Ranger; practical, tired, observant. Hands-on; tends the partner
  meadow and the field kits.
- **Ilex** — Surveyor; maps change and *timelines*, not solutions. Quietly
  unsettling, intelligent. *"Now you'll notice how often damage wears the mask
  of improvement."* Carries the recurring red-cloth symbol.

## The Protagonist

Locked from canon (§00, §07–08):
- Arrives **from Johto**, leaving behind their old home and remaining Pokémon.
  Carries *a bag, a notebook, and a memory.* **Begins with no Pokémon.**
- **"The one the scientist wrote about."** Came at the mentor's invitation.
- Arc: **witness → provisional actor → accountable participant.**
- Not auto-enrolled; becomes a Provisional Ranger only after the bridge and the
  partner-meadow (recognition, not selection — Doduo in the canon Act IV).

### Who they are (LOCKED)

- **Wren.** Young adult, 20–22. Past the threshold of childhood, already shaped
  by loss. Old enough to leave Johto on their own. Old enough to bear
  consequence.
- **Fully defined character** with a fixed name, backstory, and scripted
  internal reactions. Player chooses only **gender / pronouns** at the start
  (he / she / they). Everything else is canon.
- **Devoted protégé** of the mentor. Corresponded with them for *years* — first
  as a young naturalist writing about local sightings, then as a pen-friend,
  then as a quiet, distant student. The mentor wrote *about* them too — in an
  essay, in a journal acknowledgement, in the margins of replies — which is
  what the locals mean when they ask "are you the one the scientist wrote
  about." The player has never met the mentor in person. The whole journey
  began as the invitation that finally said *come.*

### The memory (LOCKED): a lost Smeargle named Sketch

Wren's childhood partner was a **Smeargle named Sketch** — together since they
were both small. Sketch died in Johto before the game begins, in the wetlands
on the **southern edge of the National Park, just outside Goldenrod.** Not to
violence. To a *small habitat death:* a marsh drained for a service road,
runoff fouling the pools downstream, the plants Sketch painted with vanishing
season by season. A wet spot nobody quite called a habitat. Sketch stopped
painting. Wren thought he was just tired. By the time Wren understood what was
happening, it was too late.

The wound Wren carries is the wound Delibird Isle is currently dying of, only
larger: damage at the fraying edge of a place no one is watching closely, sold
as improvement, missed until it can't be undone.

This wound is **load-bearing.**

- **The notebook is Sketch's eye, kept open.** Wren carries a notebook
  *because* their partner can no longer sketch. Every survey is a memorial in
  the only language Sketch ever spoke.
- **The Smeargle motif is intimate.** Smeargle is the region's recording mascot
  (§00). Every wild Smeargle the player meets is a quiet private gut-punch.
  Ilex's Surveyor red-cloth motif and the act of recording become deeply
  personal.
- **The mentor's letter is a direct answer to the wound.** *"You don't need to
  be brilliant. You need to be honest."* The player came because they didn't
  know enough to save their friend. They came to learn how to *see in time.*
- **Why they arrive with no Pokémon:** they cannot bring themselves to replace
  what they lost. They chose to come empty — to be a witness, not a trainer.
- **The partner-meadow becomes enormous.** When the Doduo (or other edge-species
  partner) *recognizes* them, it is not the player picking up where they left
  off. It is the player allowing themselves to walk again.
- **The climax cuts twice.** The mentor — the only person whose words ever made
  sense of the player's loss — is the same person whose lie is killing this
  region. To honor the Smeargle, the player must destroy their teacher.

### OPEN (remaining details)

- Whether Wren ever bonds with a Smeargle again — likely *no* during the main
  game; a possible late-game beat. **Defer.**

## Human antagonists (grounded, never cartoon)

- **The Industrialists** — extraction concern; power is *legitimacy*, not menace.
- **Councilman Dorsey** — political interface; sells relief/development,
  weaponizes blame, frames the Corps as outsiders.
- **Captain Harland** — proud fisher dependent on the extraction economy; misled,
  not evil; the human cost made personal.
- **Poachers / enforcers** — labor, not masterminds; cynics ("the land's already
  dead, we just admit it"), not zealots.
